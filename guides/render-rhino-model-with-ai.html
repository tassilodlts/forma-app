How to render a Rhino model photorealistically

AI rendering turns a plain Rhino viewport into a photorealistic image in seconds. The result depends far more on what you feed it than on which tool you pick. This is the workflow we use on real projects.

Updated 9 November 2026 · Amphion 3D

What image generation actually does

An image model receives a picture of your viewport (sometimes also depth and edge information) and a text prompt. It keeps the composition and repaints surfaces, light and context. It does not read your NURBS geometry. That is why the camera, the display mode and the prompt decide most of the quality.

1. Prepare the model

  1. Close the obvious gaps. Holes, missing slabs or floating objects are painted over in unpredictable ways. Run ShowEdges with naked edges on the visible parts.
  2. Give materials a colour. Even simple display colours (warm grey for plaster, brown for timber, dark grey for metal) tell the model what each surface is.
  3. Add scale objects. A few people, a car or furniture blocks fix the scale. Without them, a three storey house can come back as a doll's house.

2. Set up the camera

  1. Use a Perspective viewport with a 24 to 35 mm lens for exteriors and 18 to 24 mm for interiors (Camera panel in Properties).
  2. Put the camera at eye height, about 1.6 m, and keep verticals vertical with TwoPointPerspective. AI models reproduce converging lines faithfully, so fix them here, not in post.
  3. Save the view with NamedView so you can render the same camera again after design changes.

3. Choose a display mode

Shaded or Arctic with visible edges works best for most tools: clear surfaces, clean outlines, no confusing reflections. Pen or Technical produces a line drawing, which gives the AI more freedom (good for early concepts, worse for fidelity). Turn off grid, axes and the world axis icon before capturing.

4. Capture the input image

Use ViewCaptureToFile at 1500 to 2000 px on the long side. Most image models work internally at about 1 to 1.5 megapixels, so a 6000 px capture brings no extra detail, only longer uploads. Keep the aspect ratio you want for the final image.

5. Write the prompt

Describe what the model cannot see, in this order:

  1. Building type and materials: “three storey residential building, white lime plaster, oak windows, zinc roof”.
  2. Context: “quiet street in Madrid, plane trees, parked bicycles, people walking”.
  3. Light and time: “late afternoon, low sun from the left, soft shadows”.
  4. Photography: “architectural photography, 24 mm, eye level, natural colours”.

Avoid words that invite redesign, such as “modern”, “futuristic” or “stunning”. They make the model change your facade.

6. Keep the geometry

Every tool has some form of strength, creativity or similarity slider. For design reviews keep it low, so window positions, floor heights and roof shape stay exactly as modelled. Raise it only for mood images in early phases. If openings move or extra floors appear, the strength is too high or the prompt describes something the model does not contain.

7. Iterate and finish

  • Change one thing per run (light, season or materials) so you know what caused the difference.
  • Check every rendering against the viewport before sending it to a client: counts of windows, railings and floors.
  • Upscale only the image you keep. Small text and signage are often wrong and should be retouched or removed.

Questions

Can a Rhino model be rendered straight from the viewport?

AI image models work from an image of the viewport, not from the 3D geometry. A Rhino plugin captures the viewport for you and sends it with the prompt, so it feels direct, but the model still sees a picture.

Which display mode is the best input image?

Shaded or Arctic with visible edges gives the most faithful results. Pen or Technical line drawings give the AI more freedom and suit early concept images.

Why are my windows changed or floors added?

The strength or creativity setting is too high, or the prompt asks for something the model does not show. Lower the strength and describe only what is in the model.

What resolution should the viewport capture be?

About 1500 to 2000 pixels on the long side. Larger captures add upload time but rarely detail, because most models work at around 1 to 1.5 megapixels internally.

Are generated renderings good enough for planning applications?

It is good for mood and material studies. For documents where every dimension matters, check each image against the model and say in the caption that it is a visualisation.
