3D print an architecture model: wall thickness and scale

A building model that looks fine on screen often fails on the printer: walls too thin, mullions that vanish, meshes that are not closed. These are the numbers and checks that matter.

Updated 9 November 2026 · Amphion 3D

What printers can actually print

  • FDM (filament): walls of at least two nozzle widths, so 0.8 mm with a 0.4 mm nozzle; 1.2 mm or more for walls that should survive handling.
  • Resin (SLA, MSLA): about 0.5 mm for walls, finer for short details. Good for facades and mullions at 1:100.
  • Powder (SLS, MJF) and print services: often 0.7 to 1 mm minimum. Check the service's design rules before you upload.

Rule of thumb: whatever is below the printer's minimum at your scale is either thickened, merged into a surface, or left out. At 1:500, windows are usually just engraved or omitted; at 1:200, mullions become a thickened grid or a separate laser cut part.

Prepare the model in Rhino

  1. Copy, do not edit, the design model. A print model is a simplified version of it.
  2. Simplify. Remove interior furniture, glazing panes and anything below the minimum. Thicken what must stay (OffsetSrf with Solid=Yes for thin surfaces).
  3. Make closed solids. Every part must be watertight. Select all and check with SelOpenPolysrf or ShowEdges (naked edges). BooleanUnion overlapping walls and slabs into one solid per print part.
  4. Scale. Scale the copy to print size in millimetres. Slicers assume millimetres, so a model in metres at 1:100 would arrive as a 0.1 mm speck.
  5. Split for printing. Print floors separately if you want to lift them off and show plans, and split large models to fit the build plate. Add small locating pins or recesses.

Export STL or 3MF

  1. Export Selected as STL (binary) or 3MF. 3MF keeps units and is preferred by most current slicers.
  2. In the mesh options use a maximum distance edge to surface of about 0.01 mm for curved parts; flat buildings need very few triangles.
  3. Open the file in the slicer and check the dimensions of one known element, for example a 3 m storey should read 30 mm at 1:100.

Slicing tips for buildings

  • Print facades standing upright so window openings stay sharp, or print buildings upside down on their roof when roofs are flat.
  • Use 0.1 to 0.12 mm layer height on FDM for crisp floor lines.
  • White or light grey PLA reads best as a classic architectural model. Matte filaments hide layer lines.
  • Use the slicer's thin-wall detection (sometimes called “detect thin walls” or Arachne) to avoid gaps in walls close to the minimum.

Questions

What is the minimum wall thickness for a 3D printed architecture model?

About 0.8 mm on FDM printers with a 0.4 mm nozzle (1.2 mm recommended), about 0.5 mm on resin printers, and often 0.7 to 1 mm at print services.

What scale is best for 3D printing a building?

1:100 to 1:200 for single buildings, 1:500 for urban massing. At 1:200 and below, windows and railings need to be simplified or thickened.

Why does my slicer show the model tiny?

The model was exported in metres. Slicers assume millimetres; scale the print copy to print size in millimetres or export as 3MF, which stores units.

How do I make my Rhino model watertight?

Check for naked edges with ShowEdges or SelOpenPolysrf, close gaps, and BooleanUnion overlapping walls and slabs into one closed solid per part.

STL or 3MF?

3MF stores units and more information and is preferred by current slicers. STL works everywhere; use binary STL.
