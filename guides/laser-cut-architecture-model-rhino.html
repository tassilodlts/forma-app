Laser cut architectural model from Rhino: file setup

Most failed laser cuts are file problems, not machine problems: wrong scale, open curves, duplicate lines or walls that do not account for material thickness. This checklist gets a clean file on the first try.

Updated 9 November 2026 · Amphion 3D

1. Decide scale and material first

Everything else follows from these two numbers. Common combinations:

ScaleTypical useMaterial24 cm wall becomes
1:500Urban context, massing1 to 2 mm board or MDF0.48 mm (draw as solid blocks)
1:200Building massing with openings1 mm board, 1 to 2 mm MDF1.2 mm
1:100Facades, sections1 to 2 mm board, 2 mm acrylic2.4 mm
1:50Interiors, details2 to 3 mm MDF or plywood4.8 mm

If the scaled wall is close to a standard sheet thickness, model the walls at that thickness. Nobody can see 0.2 mm, but everyone sees a gap at a corner.

2. Build the parts at 1:1 in model space, then scale once

  1. Work from your building model. Extract each facade, floor and roof as a flat outline (DupBorder, Make2D from an orthographic elevation, or Contour for floors).
  2. Cut window openings as closed curves inside each facade outline.
  3. Scale everything once with Scale and a factor such as 0.01 for 1:100. Doing this once at the end avoids rounding errors and mixed scales.

3. Account for material thickness at the corners

Two facades meeting at a corner cannot both be full length. Choose one of these joints and apply it consistently:

  • Butt joint: long facades run through, short facades are shortened by twice the sheet thickness. Simple and robust.
  • Mitre: cleaner, but only possible with a tilting head or by hand sanding.
  • Finger joint: self-aligning, good for teaching models and acrylic.

Floors that sit inside the walls are shortened by the wall thickness on each side.

4. Layers and colours

Most labs read operations from line colour. A common convention (check yours):

  • Red (255,0,0): cut through
  • Blue (0,0,255): score or engrave line, such as window mullions or floor lines on a facade
  • Black (0,0,0): raster engrave areas, such as paving hatches

Put each operation on its own layer with that colour, and set print width to hairline if your lab prints from PDF.

5. Clean the curves

  1. SelDup and delete: duplicate lines are cut twice and burn the edge.
  2. Join all cut curves, then SelOpenCrv: every cut outline must be closed. Open curves usually mean a missing segment.
  3. SimplifyCrv converts Make2D output into lines and arcs, which cutters handle more smoothly.
  4. Remove text and dimensions unless you want them engraved.

6. Kerf and tolerances

The laser removes about 0.1 to 0.2 mm of material. For visual models ignore it. For press-fit joints, offset the cut outline outwards by half the kerf (Offset with about 0.05 to 0.1 mm), and run a small test piece first.

7. Nest on the sheet

  • Draw a rectangle the size of the sheet or laser bed and nest parts inside it.
  • Leave about 3 mm between parts and about 6 mm to the sheet edge.
  • Label each part with a small engraved number on the back face so assembly is easy.
  • Shared edges between parts save time but only when they are exactly coincident and the duplicate is deleted.

8. Export

Export the nested sheet with Export Selected as DXF (in mm, “Default” scheme) for most laser software, or print to PDF at 1:1 when your lab uses a print driver. Open the export once more and measure a known part before you send it.

Topography

For a terrain base, run Contour on the terrain surface with an interval equal to the sheet thickness divided by the scale (2 mm board at 1:500 means a contour every 1 m). Each layer is cut from one sheet; engrave the next contour line on it as a placement guide.

Questions

What file format do laser cutters need from Rhino?

Usually DXF in millimetres or a PDF printed at 1:1 with hairline strokes. Ask your lab which colours they use for cut, score and engrave.

How do I make walls fit at the corners?

Use a butt joint: long facades run through and short facades are shortened by twice the sheet thickness. Model walls at the real sheet thickness.

Why did my laser cut some lines twice?

There were duplicate curves. Run SelDup and delete the duplicates before export.

How do I make a laser cut topography model?

Run Contour on the terrain with an interval equal to the sheet thickness divided by the model scale, then cut one sheet per contour.

Do I need to compensate for kerf?

Not for visual models. For press-fit joints, offset cut outlines outwards by half the kerf, about 0.05 to 0.1 mm, and test first.
