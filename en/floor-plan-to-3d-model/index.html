Convert a floor plan into a 3D model

You have a floor plan, old or new, drawn or as an image, and you need a 3D model of it. Amphion does not build a shell of surfaces but a model of real building elements: walls, slabs, columns, windows and stairs with dimensions. From that model, Amphion then draws plans again.

From the plan to a model of building elements

Anyone who wants to convert a floor plan into a 3D model usually traces it a second time: wall by wall, opening by opening, then the heights, then the roof. For an existing building with three storeys that easily takes a day. In Amphion you start in the chat. You attach the floor plan as an image and write what Amphion needs to know: how many storeys, which floor-to-floor height, which roof. Amphion builds a model with walls, slabs, windows and stairs from it.

The result is not a picture of a house but a model you keep working with. Every wall has a thickness and a build-up, every window a size, every storey a height. You can move, change and dimension everything, in the chat or by hand.

Step by step

  1. Attach the floor plan. Put the plan into the chat as an image. A clean scan or an export from your drawing program is enough.
  2. Describe what the plan does not show. For example: “Three storeys, 3.10 m each, pitched roof, basement under the whole house.” Give one dimension you know for sure, such as the length of the street front.
  3. Check and correct. Look at the model and say what is wrong: “The kitchen wall is 20 cm further left.” Or correct it by hand with snapping and dimensions.
  4. Keep working. Draw floor plans, sections and elevations from the model, design the conversion, or send the model to consultants as IFC or DXF.

The second step matters most. A floor plan shows walls and openings but no heights. What you add decides how close the first model is to the real building. You remain the person who checks and decides; Amphion takes the tracing off your hands.

An example: the 1913 villa

On our home page we show the conversion of a 1913 villa with an extension. The existing building, the design, the interiors and the floor plans were made in Amphion, modelled in about two hours. The same model gives the state of 1913, the new ground, upper and attic floor plans, and the images.

Who it is for

  • Practices working with existing buildings: conversions, extra storeys, extensions, refurbishment
  • Competitions and early stages, when a model has to stand quickly
  • Anyone who wants to show a floor plan in 3D without drawing it a second time

Amphion runs on the Mac from macOS 14 and on Windows 10 and 11. Your plans and models stay on your computer; only the text of your chat goes to the AI.

Questions

Which plans work well?

Floor plans with clearly readable walls and openings, as a scan, a photo or an export from a drawing program. The clearer the plan, the less you need to correct afterwards.

How does Amphion know the heights?

From your description. A floor plan shows no heights, so you add the number of storeys, the floor-to-floor height and the roof shape. One reliable dimension, such as the length of a facade, helps with the scale.

Can I edit the model afterwards?

Yes. It is made of walls, slabs, columns, windows and stairs with dimensions, build-ups and layers. You change it in the chat or by hand with snapping and dimensions.

Do I get drawings back from the model?

Yes. Amphion draws floor plans, sections, elevations and detail sheets from the model, ready to print as PDF. For consultants the model goes out as IFC or DXF with layers.

Where are my plans stored?

On your computer. Projects and models stay local.
